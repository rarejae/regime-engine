## Research Question

During the 2013-2021 sustained bull market, how badly did Faber underperform passive QQQ, and would a real investor have abandoned the system before the next crisis vindicated it?

**The pattern is clear:** Faber massively outperforms during crises (+28.5% during dot-com, +26.1% during GFC, +18.9% during 2022) and modestly underperforms during bull markets (-2.7% during 2013-2021, -4.5% pre-GFC, -8.5% post-GFC recovery). The net is positive (+1.6% CAGR over the full period) because crisis protection is worth more than bull market underperformance.

**But:** Faber still returned 20.7% CAGR during 2013-2021. This is not poor absolute performance — it's only "underperformance" relative to QQQ's exceptional 23.4%. An investor earning 20.7% annually should not feel pain. The pain is relative, not absolute.

## Phase 2: The 2013-2021 Deep Dive

### 2.1 Rolling 12-Month Trailing Returns

QQQ's trailing 12-month return exceeded Faber's in **66 of 108 months (61%)**. The longest consecutive underperformance streak was **26 months** (October 2014 to November 2016).

26 months is psychologically challenging but survivable — it's roughly 2 years. The system didn't lose money during this streak, it just made less than QQQ. At the end of the streak, the investor would have been looking at ~20% annual returns and thinking "but QQQ made 25%."

### 2.2 Rolling 3-Year Trailing CAGR

QQQ's 3-year trailing CAGR exceeded Faber's in **72 of 108 months (67%)**. The worst gap: QQQ 16.9% vs Faber 8.6% (gap: +8.3%) at May 2017.

Two-thirds of the time during 2013-2021, a 3-year lookback showed QQQ winning. This is the institutional patience test — a fund manager would have been fired. A personal investor needs to understand this going in: during sustained bull markets, QQQ will look better most of the time on a trailing basis.

The "pure modern era" starting 2013: Faber 18.1% vs QQQ 18.9% — QQQ wins by 0.9% CAGR, but Faber Sharpe (1.082) beats QQQ Sharpe (0.938) by 15%. The risk-adjusted case is strong even in the worst starting period.

## Phase 4: The 2010-Era Investor

**What they built (using only 2002-2009 data):**
- Faber CAGR: 9.8% (including GFC protection — looked amazing)
- QQQ CAGR: 2.4% (QQQ was a disaster 2002-2009)
- Faber Sharpe: 0.766
- Alpha vs QQQ: +7.4% (mostly from GFC protection)

**What actually happened 2010-2026:**
- Faber CAGR: 16.5% (BETTER than their 9.8% expectation)
- QQQ CAGR: 18.0% (QQQ recovered from its 2000s disaster)
- Faber Sharpe: 0.989 (BETTER than 0.766 backtest)
- Alpha vs QQQ: -1.5% (the alpha flipped from +7.4% to -1.5%)

**The investor got better absolute returns than expected** (16.5% vs 9.8%) but saw their alpha vs QQQ evaporate (from +7.4% to -1.5%). This is the classic story of trend-following: it wins in the backtest because of the crisis, then appears to "stop working" during the bull market that follows — until the next crisis proves it works again.

**Worst rolling 12-month return:** -13.8% (ending December 2022). The investor experienced one year where they lost money — but it was 2022, when QQQ lost 32.7%.

**Months below T-bill rate:** 27 out of ~192. About 14% of rolling 12-month periods showed returns below cash. Not ideal, but 86% of the time the system beat cash.

## Phase 5: Honest Forward Distribution

| Scenario | P(est) | Faber CAGR | vs QQQ |
|----------|--------|-----------|--------|
| Bull-dominated decade | 30% | 10-13% | QQQ wins by 3-8%/yr |
| Mixed with 1 bear market | 50% | 13-16% | Faber wins by 1-4%/yr |
| Bear-heavy decade | 20% | 16-22% | Faber wins by 5-12%/yr |

**E[Faber CAGR] ≈ 13.0%** (weighted average)
**E[Faber vs QQQ] ≈ +0.5%** (Faber slightly wins on expectation, but the variance is enormous)

**Confidence in Sharpe after 3 years:** 95% CI = [-0.425, 2.283]. With 3 years of data, you cannot statistically distinguish the system from luck. Need 7+ years for the lower bound of the CI to exceed 0.

## Verdict

An investor starting in 2002 who ran Faber-Sweep-40 through March 2026 would have seen Faber **beat QQQ by 1.6% CAGR** overall, with a maximum consecutive 12-month underperformance streak of **26 months** (Oct 2014 – Nov 2016), and a maximum DCA dollar gap of **$61,942** at $700/month (Faber behind at end of 2020).

During the 2013-2021 bull market specifically: Faber returned 20.7% CAGR vs QQQ's 23.4%. **QQQ beat Faber's trailing 12-month return in 61% of months.** The 3-year trailing CAGR showed QQQ ahead 67% of the time, with a peak gap of 8.3%.

**The system survived the bull market test**, but the psychological cost is real. An investor needs to accept:
1. QQQ will look better most of the time during bull markets (61% of months on trailing 12m)
2. The DCA gap can reach $60K+ before a crisis closes it
3. Circuit breaker whipsaws cost 1-2% each and feel infuriating in real time
4. The 2020 and 2022 events justify the entire system — but you can't know they're coming
5. Faber's edge is risk-adjusted (Sharpe), not return-maximizing — it beats QQQ's Sharpe from every start date tested, but not always its raw return
