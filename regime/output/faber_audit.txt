AUDIT 1: SIGNAL ALIGNMENT — LOOK-AHEAD BUG FOUND AND FIXED

  ORIGINAL CODE:
    trend_signals_df = compute_trend_signals(prices_df)
    # Signal at index 2008-01-01 uses January 2008 end-of-month close
    # Applied on first trading day of January 2008 → sees the whole month
    RESULT: 1-MONTH LOOK-AHEAD BUG — CONFIRMED

  FIX APPLIED:
    trend_signals_df = compute_trend_signals(prices_df).shift(1)
    # Now signal at index 2008-01-01 is actually December 2007's signal
    # Applied to January returns → correct PIT alignment

  VERIFICATION:
    IVV Nov 2007: price=105.60, SMA=104.86, above=True
    IVV Dec 2007: price=104.41, SMA=105.42, above=False ← first below signal
    IVV Jan 2008: price=98.10,  SMA=105.24, above=False

    With fix: Dec 2007 "below" signal → applied to Jan 2008 (CORRECT)
    Without fix: Jan 2008 "below" signal → applied to Jan 2008 (LOOK-AHEAD)

AUDIT 5: WHY CORRECTED FABER (0.90) STILL BEATS BASELINE (0.70)

  The Faber filter's edge comes from asymmetric exposure:
  - During bull markets (IVV/QQQ above SMA 75-79% of the time): fully invested
  - During drawdowns: equity moved to cash, avoiding the worst losses
  - Net cost: ~2% annual return drag from missed reentry (lag effect)
  - Net benefit: ~10-15% drawdown avoidance during GFC/COVID/2022

  Corrected crisis drawdowns:
    GFC:   Faber +0.9% vs Baseline -26.0% (avoided -26.9pp of loss)
    COVID: Faber -11.9% vs Baseline -23.3% (avoided -11.4pp)
    2022:  Faber -8.7% vs Baseline -17.2% (avoided -8.5pp)

  The drawdown avoidance more than compensates for the return drag
  during bull markets. The Sharpe improvement (0.90 vs 0.70) is +0.20,
  which is meaningful but not the spectacular 1.76 from the bugged version.

AUDIT 6: 2008 FABER RETURN (-5.1% corrected, was +3.1% bugged)
  With the fix, Faber lost -5.1% in 2008 (vs Baseline -26.9%, IVV -36.8%).
  The 1-month lag means Faber was still invested in equity during December 2007
  and January 2008 (the first month of the crash), adding ~3-4% loss that
  the bugged version avoided by seeing the month's close before deciding.

AUDIT 7: COMPARISON TO PUBLISHED FABER
  Published Faber (2007 paper): ~0.6-0.8 Sharpe on equal-weight 5-asset universe
  Our corrected result: 0.90 Sharpe on equity-heavy 6-asset universe

  The 0.10-0.30 Sharpe premium over published results is explained by:
  (a) Higher equity weight (70% vs 20%) captures more equity risk premium
  (b) QQQ outperformed in 2002-2026 (tech secular trend)
  (c) Our period (2002-2026) had strong equity trends — favorable for SMA filters
  (d) Survivorship: we're testing on known-good ETF proxies

  The corrected 0.90 Sharpe is PLAUSIBLE and within reasonable range of
  published results. The 1.76 was not.
